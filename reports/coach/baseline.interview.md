# Pilot interview — baseline (deepseek-v4-pro)

1. The overshoot was baked in by turn 4. At range 1461 m, closure –206 m/s, 1° off-nose, and throttle still wide open, you were eating 260 m every second and had no deceleration commanded. By turn 5, at 925 m and –223 m/s, a 1° pitch correction was never going to arrest the geometry; you were arriving with nearly 250 m/s overtake on a stationary point. The tape should have started a throttle chop on turn 4 to below 0.5 and gently pitched to hold the pipper slightly high, bleeding speed below 180 m/s before the merge. Instead you flew a high-speed gun pass on a stationary target with no energy management, and the nose walked past it because your turning room was down to fractions of a second.

2. You were trying to execute a hard lead‑turn reversal to re‑acquire a target that had just slipped behind your 3/9 line, but you pulled a max‑G, sustained turn as if fighting a maneuvering bandit. The error was conflating geometry correction with combat bleeding: the balloon is stationary, so a 14.5 G pull with full mil power just buried the nose in a tightening spiral, converted all your kinetic energy into altitude loss, and kept the target outside your turn radius instead of sliding it onto the nose. The moment you saw range receding on turn 8 while still at 250 m/s, the correct answer was a lower‑G, rate‑driven slice (roll to put lift vector below the horizon and pull 4–5 G) to bring the nose around without dumping 800 m of altitude.

3. The numbers were enough through turn 5 because the geometry was simple: head‑on, constant bearing, deterministic closure. You flew a pure pursuit intercept correctly just from range, off‑nose angle, and closure. The numbers stop being enough the moment the off‑nose angle begins to increase rapidly (turn 6, from 4° to 13° in one second) with high closure — that’s a crossing-angles problem, and you need a visual picture of the aspect and the ground to judge how much vertical room you have to unload the jet and reposition. Without the picture, you defaulted to max‑pull logic, which is lethal when you can’t see the floor.

4. Add “ground truth AGL altitude (radar altimeter reading)” to the observation, displayed in tens of meters. If on turn 9 you had seen AGL dropping from ~800 m to under 200 m while G was above 12, you would have instantly unloaded, because no one willingly buries a 14 G turn into the deck against a stationary balloon. A single number showing your shrinking floor margin would have disconnected you from the pull‑harder reflex.
